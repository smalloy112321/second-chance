The Second Chance Pathway Planner

Connecticut’s Clean Slate law has erased the convictions of 170,000 people. The Second Chance Pathway Planner works for individuals or caseworkers to check records, build 30-day plans, and practice conversations that matter.

One sentence: the app tells a woman whether Clean Slate has already erased her record, then helps her use it. It works in English, Spanish, and Portuguese, with or without a caseworker.
The problem

Connecticut’s Clean Slate law (Public Act No. 21-32) was passed in May 2021 and signed into law in June 2021. While this law was meant to give people a fresh start by erasing convictions for 170,000 people, technology problems have delayed notifying these individuals. That means that many formerly incarcerated individuals are turning down jobs or apartments or checking “yes” on a conviction box over a record that no longer legally exists. This gap hits formerly incarcerated women especially hard: nearly half of the women at York Correctional Institution, Connecticut’s only women’s prison, are mothers. When a woman comes home, she is often trying to find work, secure housing, and reunify with her children all at once and many are doing it without a caseworker.

The law already helped her. Nobody told her.

What’s new

No other tool answers: “Has my record already been erased?”

What exists today

  • State websites: legal language, several steps, no notice
  • Next Steps (NC): an AI reentry planner, but not CT law and not built for women
  • 2-1-1: a list, not a plan
  • ChatGPT: can get CT rules and date math wrong and invent phone numbers
  • A clear Clean Slate answer: likely erased, erased on a date, or talk to legal aid
  • The rules run in code, not in the AI: the same facts always get the same answer
  • Built for women: children, child care, and family court are core steps
  • A Practice Room for employers, landlords, and social workers
  • With or without a caseworker, in English, Spanish, or Portuguese
The closest competitors are AI reentry planners like Next Steps in North Carolina. None uses Connecticut law, none checks Clean Slate status, and none is built around women. The biggest difference from ChatGPT: eligibility is calculated in plain Python from the state's rules, and the AI only explains the result.
How the agents work

8 AI agents + a rules engine with no AI

A fixed order run by code: predictable cost, nothing loops.

The rules engine decides each conviction’s result; the screener can’t change it.

The planner’s tools: 41 checked CT resources and the nearest Reentry Welcome Center.

The verifier checks every plan and sends it back once if needed.

All agents use gpt-6-luna through Portkey.

Safety guard first, then intake turns answers or notes into a profile, then the rules engine. The screener explains the result, the planner builds the plan with tools, and the verifier checks it. In the Practice Room, the role-play partner, coach, and summary never see her convictions.

Demo 3 · Practice Room

Rehearse the hard conversation, with a coach

Hiring manager, landlord, or social worker; friendly, neutral, or skeptical. Scored on 5 skills with a “Try saying…” rewrite. About $0.0006 per answer.

The role-play partner and coach never see her convictions. Code keeps the scores at 1 to 3 per skill and replaces any coaching that tells her to deny a record that isn't erased.

What keeps it low

  • Rules in plain Python: free
  • Cached prompt start: 45% of input at 1/10 the price
  • One questionnaire instead of a chat: 24–44 calls down to 6–11
  • Caps: 150,000 tokens per session, 2M a day
At scale: about $1.34 a month for 100 users and $134 a month for 10,000. Hosting and keeping the resource list current cost more than the AI.
Prices: 10 cents per million input tokens, 1 cent cached, 50 cents output. The honest trade-off: one inexpensive model means the verifier sends about a third of plans back once.
Safety

The worst failure: telling her “you can answer no” when she can’t

  • The rules engine makes the legal call, never the AI
  • The AI never writes phone numbers or addresses
  • The verifier labels weak plans “Needs human review”
  • The Practice Room never sees convictions
  • 36 safety tests pass
Every message goes through the safety guard first: a free keyword check in three languages, then an AI classifier. "You can answer no" is allowed only when every conviction is likely erased, and code checks it twice. Every result says it's an estimate and points to the official State Police record.
