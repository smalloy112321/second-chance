# What already exists

Checked on the web on November 9, 2026. Each line says what the tool does and what it doesn't.

| Alternative | What it does | What it doesn't do |
|---|---|---|
| **CT Clean Slate website** (portal.ct.gov/cleanslate) | The official explanation of automatic erasure, cannabis erasure, the court petition for pre-2000 convictions (JD-CR-202), the DESPP hearing, and how to get your record. | No interactive eligibility check: you read the rules and work it out yourself. No legal aid referrals, no plan, nothing for housing, IDs, or benefits. |
| **CTLawHelp.org** | Free legal information for Connecticut, including New Haven Legal Assistance's Clean Slate flyer (Aug 2025): who qualifies, the hearing and petition, and legal aid phone numbers for housing and job problems. | Static pages and PDFs. No eligibility tool, no personal plan, no pardon guide found. |
| **Calling legal aid** (New Haven Legal Assistance, Statewide Legal Services) | Free lawyers for civil problems, including housing or job denials caused by a conviction that should have been erased. | They don't handle erasure petitions or pardons. Very low income limits apply. |
| **Code for America, Clear My Record** | Began in 2016 as an online tool in California that connected about 27,000 people to legal aid; now helps states seal records automatically (about 480,000 people in California and Utah). | No longer a public eligibility checker. Not Connecticut, no plan. |
| **Pennsylvania "My Clean Slate" screener** (Community Legal Services of Philadelphia) | Pulls a person's Pennsylvania record and gives personalized information about clearing it. | Pennsylvania only. Record clearing only: no reentry plan, no practice. |
| **Michigan Legal Help, do-it-yourself expungement tool** | Checks eligibility and fills out Michigan's set-aside application. | Michigan only. Record clearing only. |
| **Next Steps by RREPS** (iOS app, Dec 2025) | Free AI-generated personalized reentry plans with progress tracking. The closest overlap. | North Carolina resources only. No record-erasure check, no practice conversations, not women-focused. |
| **Women's Recovery & Reentry App** (Why Not Prosper, Pennsylvania) | A service directory built by and for formerly incarcerated women. | A directory, not a plan; Pennsylvania; may no longer be maintained (seen only on a third-party site). |
| **Asking ChatGPT** | Answers questions in plain language, any time. | Can invent eligibility rules, dates, phone numbers, and programs. It doesn't know which Connecticut resources are open or checked, and it doesn't track a plan or practice with feedback. |

## What this app does that none of them do together

1. **A Connecticut-specific eligibility estimate from plain-Python rules** (16 tests), not from a model, with the official next step and form for each result.
2. **A 30-day plan built from 41 checked Connecticut resources**, where phone numbers and addresses come from the checked list, never from the AI, and a second agent verifies the plan before she sees it.
3. **Practice conversations with a coach** for the hard moments that follow: a job interview, an apartment application, and a reunification meeting with a social worker.
4. **Built for formerly incarcerated women**, usable alone or by a caseworker for a client.
